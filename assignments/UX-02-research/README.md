# UX-02 — Research summary (one page a stranger could act on)

## Who is the user
Caregivers (adult children / spouses) managing health appointments for family members. They are
time-poor, anxious about missing slots, and switching between clinics, portals, and phones.

## What the research said (Claude-synthesized from desktop interviews)
1. Pain is **coordination**, not discovery: they know who to call, but booking spans 2–3 systems.
2. People **fear losing a slot** — they over-book and then must cancel, which creates guilt.
3. Text confirmations beat emails; nobody checks the patient portal.
4. Trust matters: users want confirmation *before* the assistant saves a change (the source of our UX-07 “confirmation governor”).
5. A single timeline (“what’s next for mom”) is the highest-value surface.

## So we act on this
- **One assistant that confirms before booking** reduces fear of mistakes (finding 4).
- **Upcoming timeline on Home** is the primary screen (finding 5).
- **Text-style confirmations** mirror the moment of truth (finding 3).
- **Reschedule path keeps the old slot until the new one is confirmed** (finding 2) — no lost-slot anxiety.

## Deliverable check
- Summary fits on one page and a stranger could act on it → yes: five findings + four product actions above.
